KPI Tracker

An internal web app where Dealership Accelerator staff track key business metrics over time, group them into shareable Views, and see at a glance whether each metric is on target. Whoever creates a KPI or a View owns it. Access to a View is granted per person.

Status Draft v2 Owner Kevin Ferguson Updated 2026-07-09 Companion docs ui-design.html · architecture.html
This revision supersedes the earlier PRD.md. It adds Views (grouping) and per-View access, and replaces the old global viewer/editor/admin roles with an ownership + per-View sharing model.
01 · Summary

What we are building

KPI Tracker replaces the ad-hoc spreadsheet the team uses to track goals. Staff define KPIs with an explicit goal, record a value each reporting period, and read a green / yellow / red status and a trend line instantly. KPIs are organized into Views, named groupings a person owns and can share with specific colleagues. The app lives behind Google SSO at kpis.dealershipaccelerator.io and is staff-only.

Three CRUD surfaces define the product: KPIs, Views (which group KPIs), and access to Views (who can see a View). Ownership is the organizing principle: whoever creates a KPI or a View owns it and controls it.

02 · Problem & motivation

Why the spreadsheet fails

  • No clear status. Whether a number is "good" lives in someone's head or a manually colored cell. Thresholds are inconsistent and go stale.
  • No reliable history. Values get overwritten; nobody can see the trend or when a metric started slipping.
  • No access control. Anyone with the link can edit anything. There is no notion of who owns a metric or who is allowed to see a given set of metrics.
  • No structure. Every team looks at the same flat grid. There is no way to assemble "the numbers the BDC leads care about" and share just those.

We need a lightweight, access-controlled place to set goals, log periodic values, read status and trend instantly, and group metrics into Views that owners can share.

03 · Goals & non-goals

Scope

Goals (v1)

  1. Let any staff member define KPIs with an explicit, unambiguous goal.
  2. Record a value per reporting period (per-KPI cadence) and keep full history.
  3. Show each KPI's status (green / yellow / red / no-data) and trend, at a glance.
  4. Let owners group KPIs into named Views and reorder them.
  5. Let a View owner share the View, read-only, with specific colleagues.
  6. Be secure by default: Google SSO at the edge, staff-only, no public exposure.

Non-goals (v1)

  • Automated data ingestion / integrations. Values are entered by hand.
  • Alerting or notifications on status change.
  • Configurable per-KPI tolerance. The yellow band is a fixed 10% (see §6).
  • An "editor" tier on a View. Sharing is binary: Viewer (read-only) or Owner.
  • Stale-value warnings and forecasting.
  • Public or customer-facing dashboards.
04 · Users & access model

Ownership, not roles

Every user is a Dealership Accelerator staff member authenticated via Google SSO. There is no global viewer/editor/admin hierarchy. Instead, access follows ownership and per-View sharing.

Actor On things they own On things shared to them
KPI owner
creator		 Full control: edit fields & goal, record values, archive, delete. Add the KPI to any View they own. n/a
View owner
creator		 Full control: rename, add/remove KPIs, reorder, archive, delete. Manage access (grant/revoke Viewers). Can add any KPI they can access to the View. n/a
View viewer
granted		 n/a Read the View and the KPIs in it: name, values, status, trend, history. No edits, no recording values, no access changes.
App admin
config		 Break-glass oversight: manage the admin list and hard-delete. Not part of everyday flows. Seeded from a configured admin email.
Access cascade. Access is controlled at the View level. Granting someone access to a View gives them read access to every KPI in that View. A KPI is visible to a user if they own it, or if it appears in at least one View they can access. There is no separate per-KPI sharing list.

Provisioning. The first time someone signs in with a valid DA Google account they are auto-created as a normal user (owner of nothing yet). The configured seed email becomes an app admin.

05 · Core concepts

The vocabulary

Concept Definition
KPI A metric being tracked. Has a name, optional description, a unit, a goal rule, a cadence (weekly / monthly / quarterly), an owner (creator), and a history of readings.
Unit What the value is measured in, chosen from a fixed set: number, percent, or dollars. Drives how values are entered and formatted.
Goal rule How "on target" is defined: a comparator (equal to, greater than, greater than or equal to, less than, or less than or equal to) plus a single goal value. The between comparator is the exception: it takes a lower value and an upper value instead.
Reading A single value for one reporting period of a KPI (e.g. the value for "June 2026"). The current value is the reading for the latest period. Readings can be backdated to an earlier period; a backdated reading never overrides a more recent period.
Cadence How often a KPI is measured, chosen per KPI at creation: weekly, monthly, or quarterly. Determines the period key each reading is filed under and the x-axis of the trend.
Status Derived from the current value and the goal rule: green / yellow / red, or no-data when no reading exists. Never stored; always computed on the server (see §6).
View A named grouping of KPIs owned by its creator. A KPI can belong to many Views; a View holds many KPIs in an explicit order. Views are how metrics are organized and shared.
View access The list of people a View is shared with. Binary: a person is either the Owner or a Viewer (read-only). Owner manages this list.
Archive A reversible hide (soft delete) available for both KPIs and Views. Archived items drop out of the default lists and become read-only until restored.
06 · Status model

The heart of the product

Each KPI declares a goal rule: a comparator (=, >, ≥, <, ≤, or between) plus a goal value, where between takes a lower and an upper value. Status checks whether the current value satisfies the rule, with a fixed 10% tolerance band around the missed bound.

On target. The value satisfies the rule (for between, it is inside the lower-to-upper range).

Watch. The rule is missed, but by no more than 10% of the missed bound. "Close, watch it."

Off target. Missed by more than 10%.

No data. No reading recorded yet.

Rules that make it unambiguous

  • Status is computed on the server and returned by the API; the client only renders it.
  • It is computed identically everywhere it appears (dashboard, View, KPI detail).
  • Never color alone. Status is always paired with an icon and a text label for accessibility.
Rule Current value Status Why
≥ 100 100 On target satisfies the rule
≥ 100 95 Watch 5% below the 100 bound
≥ 100 80 Off target 20% below the bound
≤ 6 6.4 Watch ~7% over the 6 bound
= 50 47 Watch 6% off the target
between 5 and 10 7 On target inside the range
between 5 and 10 4.7 Watch within 10% of the 5 bound
between 5 and 10 12 Off target 20% above the 10 bound
any (none) No data no readings
07 · Functional requirements

What the system must do

7.1 · Authentication & authorization

FR-1
Only authenticated DA staff (Google SSO) can reach any part of the app. Unauthenticated requests are rejected at the edge and re-verified at the origin.
FR-2
Every action is authorized on the server by ownership and View access. The UI hides controls a user cannot use, but the server is the source of truth.

7.2 · KPIs (CRUD, owner-gated)

FR-3
Any user can create a KPI: name, optional description, a unit (number, percent, or dollars), and a goal rule. The goal rule is a comparator (equal to, greater than, greater than or equal to, less than, or less than or equal to) plus a single goal value; between instead takes a lower value and an upper value. Finally a cadence (weekly / monthly / quarterly). The creator becomes the owner.
FR-4
The goal rule is validated: every comparator needs a finite goal value; between needs a lower value and an upper value greater than it, and shows both fields; every other comparator shows only the single goal value. Invalid input is rejected with a clear message and inline form feedback before submit.
FR-5
The owner can edit a KPI's fields, unit, goal rule, and cadence, and archive / restore it. Non-owners cannot edit.
FR-6
The owner can record a value for a reporting period, defaulting to the current period and backdatable to an earlier one. Recording twice for the same period updates that period's value.
FR-7
A KPI detail view shows the goal, current value & status, full reading history, and a trend-over-time chart with the goal drawn as a reference line.
FR-7b
Every KPI card shows its trend inline. Wherever a KPI appears as a card (the dashboard and inside a View), it renders a compact sparkline of its recent readings with the goal as a faint reference line, so the direction of travel is visible at a glance without opening the detail page. A KPI with no readings shows a placeholder instead of a sparkline.
FR-7c
Each KPI card and its detail view show the KPI's owner (display name, or the email with the company domain dropped), so it is always clear who is accountable for a metric.

7.3 · Views (CRUD, owner-gated)

FR-8
Any user can create a View: name and optional description. The creator becomes the owner.
FR-9
The owner can add / remove KPIs in the View and reorder them. A KPI may belong to many Views. Removing a KPI from a View never deletes the KPI itself.
FR-10
When adding KPIs, the owner may pick any KPI they can access: KPIs they own, plus KPIs visible through Views shared with them.
FR-11
The owner can rename, archive / restore, and delete a View. A "My Views" list and a "Shared with me" list separate owned from granted Views.
FR-12
A View detail page renders the grouped KPIs as status cards in the owner's order, each with its inline trend sparkline (FR-7b), plus a roll-up count of on-target / watch / off-target / no-data.

7.4 · Access to Views (CRUD, owner-gated)

FR-13
The View owner can grant access to another staff member by email, as a Viewer (read-only). Access is binary. There is no editor tier.
FR-14
The owner can list who a View is shared with and revoke any grant. Revoking removes that person's read access to the View and to KPIs only reachable through it.
FR-15
Granting View access cascades read access to every KPI currently in the View and any added later. Only the View owner manages the access list.

7.5 · App administration

FR-16
An app admin can permanently delete a KPI or View (hard delete, irreversible) and manage the admin list. Distinct from archiving.
08 · Non-functional requirements

Quality bars

Area Requirement
Security Google SSO via Cloudflare Access at the edge; the origin re-verifies every request (signed Access JWT) and fails closed. No public inbound exposure. No secrets in the codebase. Least-privilege credentials.
Authorization Ownership and View-access checks run in server middleware on every route, never only in the UI. No unguarded API routes.
Reliability Single API instance is acceptable for v1; the database is a managed Aurora cluster with automated backups. Status and history are never silently lost.
Usability Usable by non-technical staff; status legible at a glance; follows the DAOS Design System (jet-black canvas, Sea Salt text, DA Lime for emphasis only, Inter, 8pt grid).
Performance Interactions feel instant at our scale: tens of Views, hundreds of KPIs, dozens of users, dozens of readings each.
Auditability Each KPI and View records its creator; each reading records who entered it and the period it applies to; each View grant records who granted it and when.
Quality Behavior is covered by unit, API, and end-to-end tests; changes ship through CI (lint, typecheck, tests, build) before deploy.
09 · Success criteria

How we know it worked

  1. The team stops using the tracking spreadsheet and records KPIs here instead.
  2. Anyone can answer "is this metric on target, and which way is it trending?" in seconds, without asking the owner.
  3. Teams assemble their own Views and share exactly the metrics a colleague should see.
  4. No KPI value is ever silently lost or overwritten. History is complete.
  5. Only the right people can change or see a given metric.

We are not instrumenting formal analytics in v1; success is judged by adoption and the absence of the §2 problems.

10 · Resolved decisions

Answers that shaped this spec

Question Decision
How does a KPI get its value? Periodic manual values with full history, status vs a goal + 10% band.
Reporting cadence? Per-KPI configurable: weekly, monthly, or quarterly.
Units? Fixed set per KPI: number, percent, or dollars.
Goal rule? A comparator (=, >, ≥, <, ≤, between) + a single goal value; between instead takes a lower value and an upper value.
Status logic? Value checked against the goal rule; fixed 10% yellow band around the missed bound.
View permission levels? Binary. Viewer (read-only) or Owner. No editor tier.
KPI ↔ View relationship? Many-to-many. Access granted at the View level cascades read to its KPIs.
Which KPIs can a View owner add? Any KPI they can access (owned + visible via shared Views).
11 · Future considerations

Deferred past v1

  • Status-change alerts (e.g. Slack when a KPI turns red).
  • Stale-value flag when a period's reading is overdue.
  • An editor tier on Views (co-manage membership).
  • Configurable tolerance per KPI instead of the fixed 10% band.
  • Automated data ingestion from source systems.
  • Per-reading annotations explaining a dip or spike.
